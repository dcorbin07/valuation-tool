## 2. THE FINDING THAT DECIDES WHAT THIS PROGRAM CAN EVER BE, AND IT IS FREE ARITHMETIC

**The prompt asks what each outcome would mean for an options strategy, and notes that for a call
the hurdle is the move the option price already implies, not zero. That hurdle is not close.**

A 63-session ATM call's implied move is `σ · √(63/252)`:

| implied vol | 63 sessions | the drift | drift as a share of the implied move |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25% | ±12.50pp | +1.1176pp | **8.9%** |
| 30% | ±15.00pp | +1.1176pp | **7.5%** |
| 40% | ±20.00pp | +1.1176pp | **5.6%** |

| implied vol | 126 sessions | the drift | share |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25% | ±17.68pp | +1.6367pp | **9.3%** |
| 30% | ±21.21pp | +1.6367pp | **7.7%** |
| 40% | ±28.28pp | +1.6367pp | **5.8%** |

**THE DRIFT IS BETWEEN 5.6% AND 9.3% OF THE MOVE THE OPTION ALREADY PRICES — and implied vol is
ELEVATED exactly when this strategy buys**, by a measured **+17.71%** over the name's own trailing
252-day median (`V6-OPT` stage 1), decaying only **−16.56%** by t+30.

**So `DIP-CALL` IS FORECLOSED AS AN OPTIONS PROGRAM ON ARITHMETIC ALONE, WHATEVER STEP 1 RETURNS.**
A call needs the stock to move far enough to cover the premium; a 1.1–1.6pp drift against a ±12–28pp
implied move cannot do it at any strike or tenor on the grid. **This is not a power statement and no
amount of data changes it.**

**THE CONSEQUENCE, AND IT IS THE MOST USEFUL SENTENCE IN THIS DRAFT: IF LEAD 1 IS REAL, ITS
TRADEABLE FORM IS SHARES, NOT CALLS.** Don's original idea was a call; the arithmetic says the
same insight, if it survives, is a **stock** holding. That is worth knowing before anyone spends an
options trial — and it is why §3 below registers an **equity** arm and **no option arm at all**.

The round-trip cost is paid **per trade**, so a longer holding period pays it fewer times. Taking

**The 21-session cell fails on cost — which is exactly what `DIP-CALL-2` measured — and the 63- and

## 4. FOR DON — in plain words

**What is being tested.** One thing only: after a sharp one-day drop in a big company, the stock
keeps drifting **up** for three to six months. We saw that on 2009–2019 and we are not allowed to
believe it from the window where we found it, so the test is the **dot-com-and-2008 decade**, which
this program has never looked at.

**And the most important thing I found, which needed no new data.** **A call cannot capture this,
ever.** The drift is about **1.1pp over three months**, while a three-month option already prices
in a move of **±12 to ±20pp** — the drift is **6–9%** of what you are paying for, and the option is
*more* expensive than usual precisely because the stock just fell. **So your original idea — buy a
call after a sharp drop — is arithmetically foreclosed, and no amount of data changes it.** What
survives is the same insight expressed in **shares**, which nets roughly **+1.8%/yr** on our own
cost assumptions because you only trade four times a year instead of twelve.

**The second lead is closed and will not come back.** "The market rises after these days" would
need **53 to 104 years** of data to establish; we have **27**. It is also partly an illusion of the
window: 2009–2019 was the longest bull market on record, so *any* rule that buys after down days
looks good there without predicting anything.

**Realistic odds.** I would put the drift surviving 1999–2008 at **20–25%**, and surviving in a form
worth trading at **10–15%**. The reasons are specific: the horizon shape we found was the **opposite**
of the published effect the test was built on, which usually means a window artefact rather than a
discovery; the margin is thin (**+1.8pp/yr** before any slippage beyond the modelled floor); and the
era may not even carry enough events — there is a real chance the register **refuses to run**, which
would cost nothing.

**Is it a fair test?** For lead 1, **yes, with one disclosed blemish**: 1999–2008 is genuinely
untouched by this program and needs no data we lack, but `POOL-SIZE` has already read that decade
once for a different question, and it is **one read** — after this, the era is spent for DIP-CALL
too. For lead 2, **no fair test exists**, which is why it is closed rather than queued.
