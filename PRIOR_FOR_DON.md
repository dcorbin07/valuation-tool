## WHAT THE PRODUCT ACTUALLY DOES TODAY

`INDEX-BOOK` measured the book the site actually serves — the $10B tier, score-weighted, 8% cap,
0.30 band — net of costs:

* **+1.95pp/yr over SPY** across 2009–2026.
* **+0.27pp/yr over SPY in the recent half.**
* Roth **17.16%/yr** against taxable **12.20%/yr** — so the tax wrapper is worth **4.96pp/yr**,
  and **the same book held in a taxable account is 3.03pp/yr BELOW SPY.**

Two sentences about the same book are both true and neither is allowed to travel alone: it is
**−0.06pp against the all-cap equal-weighted universe** and **+4.12pp against its own $10B tier**.

**So the honest summary of fifteen months of work is: a book that has beaten SPY by about two
points a year gross of nothing and net of trading cost, mostly in the first half, and only because
it is in a Roth.**

## WHAT SIZE OF EDGE IS PLAUSIBLE

**For a long-only book of large US stocks, after trading cost: 0 to 2 percentage points a year.**
Not five. Not ten.

Three reasons, all from our own measurements rather than from reading:

1. **Our own best out-of-sample evidence is small.** `X8` mapped the composite onto an independent
   vendor's international data with **no tuning at all** and got **+2.05%/yr in Japan** and
   **+3.36%/yr in developed Europe** — and the **USA was the weakest region tested**. Those are
   long-short factor spreads at capped value weights, which is a *different and generally easier*
   object than a long-only book, and they still only reached two to three points.
2. **The project's own headline does not clear its own multiple-testing bar.** The long-short HAC
   *t* is **2.6199** against a Harvey-Liu-Zhu hurdle of **3.3255** at today's 252 equity trials.
   The hurdle only ever rises.
3. **Costs are real and they are the biggest lever we control.** The served large-cap book trades
   at **9.58 bps** one-way; the published all-cap decile at **33.35**; a concentrated all-cap book
   at $1M was measured at **87 bps**. At the served book's 2.44 turnover, that is a spread of about
   **0.2pp to 2.1pp a year** in cost alone — comparable to the entire edge.

## WHAT WOULD COUNT AS SUCCESS

**Success is +1 to +2pp/yr over SPY, net of cost, on a book the scan can build, that survives the
pre-2009 holdout and keeps doing it forward.** That is a good outcome and it is worth having.

**It is not a dramatic outcome, and the honest thing to say about it is that it will be hard to
tell apart from luck for a long time.** The forward contract measured this directly: at the book's
tracking error the meter has **13.3% power at 60 months** and needs about **19pp/yr** to cross at
five years. A real 2pp edge takes roughly **30 years** to reach *t* = 2 against SPY. **The
pre-2009 period is the only sample we can get that is anywhere near long enough — and per your
Addition 2 it is now TWO eras, ~1972-1989 and 1990-2008, each read ONCE. Splitting ~37 years in
two buys REPLICATION at the price of resolution: each era alone resolves about 2.8-3pp/yr rather
than the ~2pp a single block would. That is the right trade, and it is why neither era may be
spent casually.**

## WHAT WOULD COUNT AS FAILURE, STATED NOW SO IT CANNOT BE ARGUED LATER

* Either pre-2009 era shows the frozen five-theme book at or below the market, net of cost - and
  if the two eras disagree, the honest word is NOT_REPLICATED rather than a choice between them.
* Or the recent half's **+0.27pp** continues forward for a few more years.

**Either of those means the answer is "this does not beat an index fund", and that is a perfectly
respectable result for fifteen months of careful work.** The record's value in that case is the
252 arms that said no — which is a real contribution and is the thing almost nobody does.

## THE ONE THING THAT GENUINELY LOOKS LIKE AN OPPORTUNITY

`INDEX-BOOK` took the gap between the served book and the published decile apart one knob at a
time. The tier step costs **−5.99pp/yr**, and about **70%** of that — **−4.18pp** — is, in that
row's own words, *"the small-cap premium the tier declines to hold."*

**The product refuses that return because of capacity. A Roth book does not have a capacity
problem.** `P2` measured the crowding point at a **$5.1B cohort**, which is simply not binding at
the size you actually run. That is the one place in this record where a measured, several-point
return is being left on the table for a reason that does not apply to you.

**It is also mostly BETA rather than skill** — a small-cap tilt, not alpha — and `R1`'s re-run
found the size loading **not statistically significant** on the corrected panel. So it is a factor
bet with a defensible rationale, not an edge. **Worth testing, worth owning honestly, and not
worth describing as an edge if it turns out to be a tilt.**

## THE DISCIPLINE THAT MAKES ANY OF THIS WORTH BELIEVING

Four things, in `RESEARCH_CHARTER.md`:

1. **One metric**: net-of-cost return of a buildable book, Roth, vs SPY.
2. **Every number names its book** — the failure that put a +32%/yr gross figure for a book nobody
   holds on a public site.
3. **Locked holdouts nobody looks at** until a construction is frozen: two pre-2009 eras, each
   read once, plus a 2x2 build/check grid (2009-2019 x half the tickers, checked once on
   2020-2026 x the other half) for anything new.
4. **A cap of twelve further arms.** 252 arms produced one product result of +1.95pp/yr. More arms
   on the same seventeen years buy hurdle, not knowledge.
