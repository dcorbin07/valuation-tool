# 04 — Compliance Checklist
*Informational research, not legal advice. The documents that decide this are Atlantic Union's own code of conduct and outside-activity / social-media policies, which I have not seen. This list exists so the conversation with HR or Compliance is short and well prepared.*

## Before recording anything

**Confirm you hold no registrations.** You said none; check BrokerCheck (brokercheck.finra.org) under your name anyway, because it is the first thing a compliance officer will do. If any Series 6/7/63/65 registration exists through an affiliate, FINRA Rules 3270 (outside business activities) and 3280 (private securities transactions) apply directly and a monetised channel is a reportable OBA requiring prior written notice. FINRA has proposed replacing those with Rule 3290, filed with the SEC in January 2026 with a decision expected around the end of Q3 2026; under it a non-investment-related activity like a football channel would not need notice at all, while a finance channel still would. If unregistered, none of that applies, but read on.

**Read the code of conduct for four things:** the outside-employment / outside-activity approval process (large banks route this through HR with manager sign-off); any clause prohibiting acting as an "investment advisor" or giving financial advice outside the bank (Truist's public code has exactly that clause, and AUB's may too); the personal social-media rules (typically: no bank branding, no customer or confidential information, a "views are my own" disclaimer, no statements to media on the bank's behalf); and anything about outside compensation.

**File the outside-activity request in writing and keep the approval.** Suggested scope language, adjust to their form:

> A personal YouTube channel about fantasy football analysis (weekly start/sit recommendations, statistical analysis of NFL data, educational content on probability and decision-making applied to fantasy sports). The channel will not reference Atlantic Union Bank, its products, customers or business; will not discuss securities, banking products or provide financial advice; will carry a disclaimer that views are my own and the channel is unaffiliated with my employer; and any future sponsorships will be disclosed in accordance with FTC guidelines. Time commitment approximately 10–15 hours per week outside working hours. Revenue, if any, from platform ad revenue, memberships and sponsorships unrelated to financial services.

If you later want the finance-principles series to include anything about markets or investing, treat that as a **separate** request with its own scope ("general, impersonal educational content; no individualised advice; no securities recommendations; no affiliate compensation from brokerages, lenders or crypto platforms").

## On the channel, every video

Description and banner: your name; "I work at a bank; this channel is a personal project and is not affiliated with or endorsed by my employer"; "nothing here is financial advice." Sponsorships and affiliate links disclosed **in the video itself**, audibly and on screen near the start, and again in the description near the link — the FTC's Endorsement Guides say a description-only disclosure is not "clear and conspicuous," "#ad" early in a caption works, "affiliate link" alone does not, "I get commissions for purchases made through links" does.

## Fantasy vs betting — the line that matters

Fantasy analysis (rankings, start/sit, waivers) is commentary. The friction appears with sportsbook money. YouTube prohibits linking to gambling sites not certified by Google and any "guaranteed returns" language, and since March 2025 age-restricts content that promotes or depicts online gambling, including from certified sites, with an exception for certified sportsbook promotion during live sporting events; age-restricted videos lose most ads. In Virginia, a person compensated per registration, per depositing registration or on a revenue share for directing business to a sports-betting operator is an "affiliated marketer" who must be registered as a sports betting vendor (11VAC5-70-10 and -80), and Virginia's advertising standards require responsible-gaming messaging and forbid claims that betting guarantees success. DFS and fantasy-platform sponsors (Sleeper, Underdog's pick'em products are a grey area — check which state licence they operate under) are the clean lane. Discussing lines and props as *model inputs* is fine; "bet this" is not the channel.

## The finance-content rules, if and when

The Investment Advisers Act excludes bona fide publishers of general, regular, impersonal commentary (Lowe v. SEC, 1985). What moves a creator off that line: individualised answers ("you with your $40k should…"), paid communities with tailored guidance, managing anyone's money, or touting. Section 17(b) of the Securities Act requires disclosing any compensation for promoting a security; the SEC's 2022 case against Kim Kardashian ($1.26M) was an undisclosed $250k promotion. "Not financial advice" is best practice with no legal force; the disclosures that matter are who you are, that you are not licensed, that the content is general, and what you are paid for.

## Career optics

A named, sourced, corrected fantasy-analytics channel with a public ledger reads as a data-engineering portfolio to the analyst roles you are targeting. A market-prediction channel by a branch banker reads differently to those employers' compliance functions. Keep the two separate in scope and in timing, and put the engine's repo on the GitHub profile that is already on your resume.
