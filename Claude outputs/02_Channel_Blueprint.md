# 02 — Channel Blueprint
*The operating plan for the football channel. Read `01_Channel_Decision_Brief.md` first for why.*

## Positioning

One sentence, for the banner and for you: **the only fantasy football channel that publishes its model's every call, wins and losses both, and shows you the math when it is wrong.** The persona is not "expert with the answers"; it is a physicist-turned-banker who built a stock-research lab whose job was to disprove his own picks, and is now doing the same thing to fantasy football in public. Skeptical, specific, willing to be wrong on camera, allergic to "lock of the week."

The finance brain is the flavour, not the topic. Expected value, variance, order statistics, Kelly, correlation, regression to the mean, base rates and calibration are the recurring tools, applied to lineups. That framing is what nobody else in the niche has, it is evergreen (the offseason series), and it keeps a door open to finance content later under the same persona.

## Name

The brand should be the channel, with you as the host, so it can outgrow you or be separated from you later (that also makes the employer disclosure cleaner: a named show with a stated scope). Candidates, in my order of preference, none checked for handle or trademark availability — do that before printing anything:

**The Fantasy Ledger.** Says the whole premise: every call logged. "The Ledger" is already the name of the accountability segment and the public accuracy page, so brand and product share a name. Weak point: sounds a little dry until the on-camera personality fixes that.

**Lineup Lab.** Pairs with valquo's Edge Lab; "lab" signals testing rather than telling. Check for existing DFS tools using the name.

**The Bench Press.** Memorable, funny, and "press" reads as a publication. Risk: the fitness pun goes stale, and search results will be about lifting.

**Expected Points.** A real football-analytics term with a finance double meaning. Likely contested.

Whichever you choose, your name goes in the channel description and the on-camera intro, not in the channel name.

## Format

On camera, talking head, with charts that come straight out of the engine. That last part is the visual signature: every chart on screen is generated by the same code that made the call, so the visuals are cheap for you and impossible for a personality channel to copy. Target length 8–14 minutes for the weekly shows (the observed sweet spot for analysis content; retention decides, not a rule). Shorts are a discovery tool, not a revenue plan: one to two a week, each a single testable claim ("target share predicts next week five times better than touchdowns; here's the plot"), 25–40% of output at most.

Two fixed weekly slots in season, because the big shows publish five to seven pieces a week with teams and you have 10–15 hours:

| slot | publish | length | what it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Call** | Thursday evening (Thursday-night game excluded and noted) | 10–14 min | The engine's start/sit for the week, organised around the **disagreements with consensus** — that is where the information is and where the ledger gets scored. Confidence shown as a distribution, not a number. One deep dive on a mechanism. |
| **The Ledger** | Monday night or Tuesday morning | 6–10 min | What hit, what missed, why; the running record versus consensus; calibration check ("we said 70%, we hit 64%"); one waiver angle driven by opportunity shifts the engine saw. Never skipped. A skipped ledger is the one thing that would break the brand. |

The Ledger also lives as a page (a published artifact or a page on the engine's repo) that updates weekly, so the record is checkable without watching.

## The weekly rhythm at 10–15 hours

| when | hours | task |
|---|---|---|
| Mon evening | 2–3 | Stats final enough to score the week; run the ledger script; record and cut The Ledger |
| Tue | 1 | Verify the pipeline is breathing; review captures; note the week's news |
| Wed | 1–2 | Engine run at the `publish` clock for Thursday's show; pick the deep-dive |
| Thu | 3–4 | Script (bullet outline, not prose), record, edit, package The Call |
| Fri–Sat | 1–2 | One Short; A/B a thumbnail; answer comments; register any new hypothesis as a PREREG before touching code |
| Sun | 0.5 | Watch football. The pregame capture runs itself. |

That is 9–13 hours. Engine development (models, harness) is a separate budget and lives mostly in the offseason; in season the engine is deliberately simple (`PREREG_DRAFT_002`).

## The offseason (February–August)

This is where football channels die, and where your positioning is strongest, because the finance-principles series is evergreen and does not need games:

The **"Fantasy is a portfolio" series** (six to eight videos, one every two weeks): expected value and why your lineup is an order statistic, not a sum; variance — when to start the boom/bust player; correlation and stacking; Kelly and roster construction; regression to the mean and the touchdown trap; base rates and why ADP is a market; calibration — what "70% confident" has to mean; survivorship bias in "league-winners."

The **build-in-public series**: what the census found; the first arm's read; the myths the data killed (defence-vs-position, primetime, revenge games, weather); how the capture pipeline works and why the timestamps matter.

Draft season (July–August): the engine's draft-side outputs, best-ball and dynasty only if you enjoy them; a public "Ledger League" on Sleeper where viewers play against the engine's lineup each week is a cheap, sticky community feature and a natural sponsor slot for Sleeper itself.

## Packaging discipline

Title and thumbnail get 30% of the time, not 5% — the creators who grow say the difference between a good and a great video is mostly packaging. Every title makes a specific claim or asks a specific question that the video answers; "Week 5 Start/Sit" is a label, "The model disagrees with everyone on 4 players this week" is a promise. First 30 seconds: the disagreement, the stakes, then the intro. Use Studio's A/B title testing on every video. Watch the retention "intro" number; if under 60–70% after 30 seconds, the open is wrong, not the topic.

## Gear and tools

At about $300: the phone you own on a tripod, a wireless lavalier, one LED key light with a softbox, and a quiet room; sound and light before camera, every guide agrees. At about $1,000: a Sony ZV-E10 or ZV-1, a Shure MV7 or Rode NT-USB+, an Elgato Key Light or Godox softbox, and a cheap teleprompter app (Teleprompter.com or PromptSmart) for the scripted openers. Edit in DaVinci Resolve (free). Charts come from the engine via matplotlib with a fixed house style (one palette, one font, dark and light variants); Manim for the occasional finance-principles animation once the series starts. Descript is worth it if filler-word cleanup is eating your Thursday.

## Disclosures, on every video

In the description and in the channel banner: your name; that you are a bank employee and this is a personal project not affiliated with your employer; that nothing here is financial advice (the finance-principles series stays general and impersonal — no individual portfolio questions answered, ever); that any sponsorship or affiliate link is disclosed in the video itself, out loud and on screen, not just in the description (the FTC's stated position). No sportsbook affiliate codes unless and until you have looked at Virginia's sports-betting vendor registration rules; DFS and fantasy-platform sponsors are the clean lane.

## Growth targets and pre-committed re-reads

Copied from the brief so they live with the plan: by 2027-02-15, at least 24 videos, the ledger public every week from Week 4, 300 subscribers as the base case and 500 as the stretch. Format re-read if under 20 videos; packaging season if videos shipped but growth is flat; the niche is re-examined only at the 2027 season read, next to the engine's own verdict. Partner Program: plan for the February 2027 bar of 1,000 subscribers and 8,000 watch hours; do not build the plan around ad revenue before 2028.
