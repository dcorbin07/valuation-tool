# 01 — Channel Decision Brief
*Prepared 2026-09-15 for Don. Research memos and sources are in `05_Research_Notes_and_Sources.md`; the engine design is in `03_Engine_Design_Spec.md`; the running code is in `ff-engine/`.*

## The recommendation

**Build the football channel, and make the engine the product.** Position it as the one fantasy channel with a public, pre-registered, audited record — "fantasy football run like a quant fund" — hosted by you, under your name, on camera. Start capturing data this week (the pipeline in `ff-engine/` already runs), treat the 2026 season as a paper season with a public ledger from about Week 4, and decide on a paid tier after the season on a verdict you committed to in advance. Keep the finance channel as a sequenced option for after you leave retail banking, not as the launch.

That is not the direction with the best ad rates. It is the direction where your actual advantages (the valquo protocol, the quant tooling, a physicist's comfort with uncertainty, and an on-camera personality you are willing to put your name on) are rarest, where nobody on YouTube is doing the specific thing, where the employer and regulatory friction is lowest, and where income has a product path rather than an ad-and-affiliate path. The reasoning follows; the counter-case is at the end because it is real.

## What your answers ruled in and out

You want income and enjoyment, you will go on camera under your own name, you can give it 10–15 hours a week, and you hold no securities licenses but have not read Atlantic Union's outside-activity policy. Three consequences:

Being on camera under your own name as a branch banker is the single fact that hurts the finance option most. A finance channel's biggest revenue line for its biggest creator was brokerage and crypto affiliate links (Andrei Jikh disclosed $1.75M of affiliate income against $1.16M of ads in 2021), and that is precisely the line a bank's code of conduct and the SEC's anti-touting rules make dangerous. Large-bank codes (Truist's is public) require manager and HR approval of any outside activity and flatly prohibit "acting as an investment advisor" outside the bank. A branch employee whose customers can recognise him giving market commentary is the case compliance officers are paid to notice. You can probably get a general, impersonal, no-affiliates finance channel approved — but you have not asked yet, and you should not build a launch on an answer you do not have.

Ten to fifteen hours a week is enough for one substantial long-form video plus one lighter one and the engine's weekly operations, once capture is automated. It is not enough for a research-journalism channel, where the creators you named describe hundreds of hours per video and multi-person teams.

Income means the plan needs a path to money that does not depend on AdSense in year one. For every direction, ads in the first 12–18 months are close to zero; the difference is what else exists.

## Why finance is second, not first

The case for it is real: your credentials are finance credentials, the RPMs are the best of the three, and there is a great video sitting in your valuation-tool folder — "I spent a year and 224 pre-registered trials trying to prove my own stock picker works; here is what survived." Patrick Boyle and Ben Felix, the credentialed on-camera experts, are the sub-segment still compounding while the mass-market personalities have stalled. Casual Finance went from zero to roughly 350,000 subscribers in about thirteen months on skeptical macro essays. There is room for a physicist-banker who is honest about what does not work.

The case against it, today, is specific rather than general. First, the compliance question is unanswered, and on camera under your own name it is not a formality. Second, the content you described — how world events and government decisions will affect markets in the short and medium term, and how to prepare — is event-timed commentary, which sits closer to the "tipster" side of the Supreme Court's publisher exclusion (Lowe v. SEC) than a regular-schedule educational channel does, and it invites individualised questions in the comments that you must not answer. Third, the money in the niche flows through affiliates, and affiliates are the exposure. Fourth, you are trying to move into analytical finance roles at places like the Richmond Fed, CarMax and Capital One; a public market-prediction channel is something their compliance functions will read closely, while a data-engineering portfolio is something their hiring managers will read happily. None of that kills the idea. It sequences it: once you are out of the branch (or once compliance signs off in writing on a general, no-affiliate format), the same on-camera persona can carry finance content, and the football channel will have taught you how to make videos.

## Why journalism is last

Every legacy explainer channel in the research set except two is flat or declining in subscribers, and the two that are growing are a foundation-funded newsroom with about thirty staff (More Perfect Union) and a Vox alumna with a four-person team (Cleo Abram). YouTube itself says the news audience is huge and growing, and that the growth is going to established journalists moving over. You would be entering with no beat, no credential in the subject, no team, and 10–15 hours a week against creators who describe four-month production cycles. It is the direction with the weakest fit to what you bring, and it is the one where "keeping people informed" carries the most ad-suitability risk on sensitive topics.

## Why football wins for you, and what is honestly wrong with it

The reference channels that grew biggest are personalities (Grossi, Perna) or film-breakdown teachers (Kollmann, O'Sullivan). The data-product companies have small YouTube footprints relative to their brands — their channels are funnels to $55–$300-a-year subscriptions. Nobody in the set pairs a YouTube audience with a transparent, backtested model and a season-long public ledger of calls, misses and calibration. The closest things — Subvertadown's accuracy reports, 4for4's published record, FantasyPros' accuracy competition — are websites. FantasyPros runs a public in-season accuracy competition with 100-plus experts, scored on an "accuracy gap" per rank slot, and anyone who publishes rankings through their platform is automatically entered. That is an external, audited scoreboard you can put your model on from year one, for free.

Your protocol is the product. The valquo README's line — most of what the lab produced are rejections and nulls, and that is the reason to trust the rest — is a stronger content engine in fantasy than in stocks, because fantasy is full of confidently stated folklore that data can test on camera every week: defence-vs-position matchups, "revenge games," weather, primetime, rest advantage, target-share persistence versus touchdown regression. Each test is a video whether it passes or fails, and the failures are funnier.

Now the honest problems. Ad rates are the worst of the three, so ads will never be the business; at sports RPMs, 100,000 monthly views is on the order of $100–$300 a month. Growth timelines in this niche are long: Kollmann described three years of flatline before the hockey stick, Grossi took about five years to 100,000 subscribers, and Tim Jenkins started in 2018 and sits at 52,000 in 2026. The season is seven months long and the income swing is roughly 2× in-season versus off; you need an evergreen plan for February through August. Sportsbook money — the richest sponsor category — triggers both YouTube age-restriction rules and, in Virginia, a sports-betting vendor registration for affiliate marketers, so the low-friction version takes DFS and fantasy-platform sponsors and leaves sportsbook affiliate codes alone. And the engine's most likely first finding is that it roughly matches consensus on point projections, because the crowd is strong: the best weekly projections explain only 3–23% of the variance in outcomes, averaging sources beats individual sources in 63% of head-to-heads, and the one public hierarchical model with opponent terms did not beat a seven-game rolling average out of sample. Your channel's story cannot depend on the engine winning. It has to be robust to a null — which, given how you built valquo, is the story you are best at telling.

## The economics, without inflation

Year one, all directions: assume ad revenue of roughly nothing. The Partner Program requires 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 watch hours in twelve months today, and YouTube announced on August 10 that from February 1, 2027 new applicants need 8,000 watch hours (or 20 million Shorts views in 90 days); existing partners are grandfathered. A channel starting now will almost certainly not clear the current bar by January 31, so plan for the doubled one. Realistically ads begin in 2028.

Where football income can actually come from, in order of plausibility: a paid tier of the engine (league-specific start/sit via the Sleeper API, full distributions, the calibration dashboard) at the $5–$15 a month price point the niche already pays 4for4 and ETR, launched for the 2027 season **only if** the 2026 paper season earns it on the pre-committed verdict; channel memberships and a Discord once there is an audience that wants the weekly ledger early; DFS and fantasy-platform sponsorships once the channel is in the tens of thousands of subscribers (Kollmann's disclosed peak was up to $40,000 a month in season, two-thirds from sponsors and merch, at roughly 400,000 subscribers — that is the ceiling of the niche for a solo creator, not the median). Two licensing facts bind the product path: Sleeper's API is free for non-commercial use only, and FantasyPros' free API tier is non-production. Talking about what the engine found is fine; selling its output needs a licence conversation with Sleeper and a paid data plan. That is a 2027 decision and it is written into the engine's ledger as one.

## The one thing with a clock on it

The free injury data lost its timestamps after the 2024 season; the 2026 file has no `date_modified` column at all. The lines in the free schedule file are overwritten by closing lines once games are played. The Wayback Machine's nearest capture of the NFL injury page to a random Saturday in October 2025 was ten months away. So a point-in-time backtest of any 2025-or-later week — the thing that separates a rigorous engine from a spreadsheet — is only possible for weeks someone captured live. Every week that passes uncaptured is gone. The capture pipeline in `ff-engine/` was built and run today; the nflverse side captured and verified end to end, and the API adapters need their first run from your machine (the workspace that wrote them cannot reach Sleeper or Open-Meteo). Deploying it to the Oracle VM this week is the first task regardless of anything else in this brief, and it is register `PREREG_000` in the engine's ledger.

Weeks 5–18: publish the ledger every week without exception; enter the FantasyPros accuracy competition if the platform's requirements allow; run the census register once ten weeks are captured; use the Shorts feed for one-fact-per-clip tests of fantasy folklore.

January–February 2027: first read of the forward track on the pre-committed rule (a read, not a verdict, unless the effect is large either way); post-mortem video; decide on the paid tier for 2027 on that read.

February–August 2027: the evergreen "finance principles for fantasy" series (expected value, variance and why the lineup is an order statistic, Kelly and roster construction, correlation and stacking, regression to the mean, base rates in ADP), draft-season tools, and the build-in-public engine series. This is also when you revisit finance content if compliance has cleared it.

## Pre-committed re-read criteria for the channel itself

In your own idiom: thresholds before the run, or the result is a judgement call. By 2027-02-15, the end of season one: at least 24 videos published, the ledger public every week from Week 4 with zero skipped weeks, and 300 subscribers as the base case with 500 as the stretch. Base rates are sobering (40.6% of channels ever reach 1,000 subscribers and 1.3% reach 100,000; median growth for active channels is 0.5–1.3% a month), so fewer than 300 subscribers is **not** a kill signal on its own. The pre-committed re-read is on the *format*: if fewer than 20 videos shipped, the rhythm was wrong for your week and gets redesigned; if 24 shipped and the ledger held but growth is flat, the packaging gets a season of deliberate A/B work before the niche is questioned. The niche itself is re-examined only at the 2027 season read, alongside the engine's verdict.

## What would change my mind

If Atlantic Union signs off in writing on a general, impersonal, no-affiliate finance channel and you are content to forgo affiliate income, direction A's economics are better and the compliance gradient flattens; a hybrid — a football-first channel that runs the finance-principles series in the offseason — becomes the natural bridge rather than a pivot. If you cannot sustain two videos a week through November while the W&M certificate is running, football's seasonality turns from a feature into a liability and a lower-cadence finance essay channel fits the calendar better. And if the census register comes back showing consensus is far weaker in your scoring format than the literature says, the engine's ceiling rises and the paid tier moves earlier.
